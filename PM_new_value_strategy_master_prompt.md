# 6. Source discipline and research rules

## 6.1 Primary source hierarchy

The focal paper is the source of truth for its sample, variables, tests, numbers, and claimed contribution. Read it completely before drafting the slides.

For each quantitative claim, identify:

- Exact table or figure.
- Printed article page and PDF page if they differ.
- Unit, portfolio definition, sample period, and weighting method.
- Whether the number is raw, risk-adjusted, a regression coefficient, a t-statistic, or a model-test statistic.

Do not infer a page number. If uncertain, cite the table/section and state that the PDF page needs confirmation.

## 6.2 External literature research

Search recent literature through the current date. Prefer:

1. Journal or publisher pages.
2. DOI records.
3. NBER, SSRN, university repositories, and author-hosted manuscripts.
4. Official working-paper pages.

Clearly label working papers, preprints, and non-peer-reviewed evidence. Do not present a recent working paper as settled evidence.

Use external literature only when it helps one of these jobs:

- Position the paper’s contribution.
- Explain the theoretical/accounting mechanism.
- Identify a credible limitation.
- Propose a specific future test.
- Prepare a likely Q&A defense.

Do not turn the presentation into a broad literature review.

At minimum, verify and consider these research anchors:

- Fama and French on book-to-market/HML and factor models.
- Ball et al. on accruals, cash flows, and cash-based operating profitability.
- Baolian Wang, **“A New Value Strategy”**, Review of Asset Pricing Studies, 2024, DOI `10.1093/rapstu/raad014`.
- Research on intangible-adjusted profitability or value measures.
- Recent work on out-of-sample asset-pricing model evaluation.
- Evidence on UK trading costs, stamp duty, bid–ask spreads, and short-sale costs.
- Research using break-even transaction costs or implementability tests for factor strategies.

## 6.3 No fabricated EDA

The focal paper uses proprietary or restricted datasets such as LSPD and Datastream and does not supply a complete raw replication dataset.

Therefore:

- Do not claim to have replicated the paper unless the actual underlying data are available.
- Do not generate synthetic observations and call them evidence.
- Do not infer time series that are not in the paper.
- Do not add visually precise but unsupported data points.
- If raw data are unavailable, reconstruct only values explicitly reported in tables or text.
- Public contextual data may be used only when clearly labeled **“context, not replication.”**
- If no quantitative data support a proposed chart, use a conceptual process diagram instead.

## 6.4 Evidence language

Use disciplined verbs:

- “finds,” “reports,” or “documents” for the paper’s results.
- “is consistent with” for interpretation.
- “does not establish” for limitations.
- “could be tested by” for extensions.

Avoid unsupported causal language such as “proves,” “causes,” or “guarantees.”

# 7. Facts to verify against the focal paper

The following are working extraction notes. Verify every item against the original paper before using it. If any conflict exists, the paper wins.

## 7.1 Sample and design

- UK accounting universe approximately June 1995 to June 2024.
- Return tests approximately July 2000 to June 2024.
- Roughly 1,700 non-financial London Stock Exchange firms, including delisted firms where available.
- Foreign firms, financial firms, observations with missing required fields, and firms with negative book value are excluded as specified by the paper.
- Portfolios are formed annually around July.
- Stocks are sorted into deciles.
- Monthly value-weighted returns are measured for the following twelve months.

## 7.2 Construct

Verify the paper’s precise notation and signs for the cash-based operating profitability measure. The working interpretation is:

`CBOP = operating profit + Δ deferred revenue + Δ accrued expenses + Δ accounts payable − Δ prepaid expense − Δ inventory − Δ accounts receivable`

The new value signal is CBOP scaled by market equity, labeled in the paper as CBOP/M or an equivalent notation.

The presentation must explain the intuition before presenting the equation:

- Accounting earnings contain accrual components.
- Working-capital accruals may be less persistent or more exposed to estimation and discretion.
- Adjusting operating profit toward a cash-based measure may better capture recurring economic profitability.
- Scaling by price/market value connects profitability to what the investor pays.

Do not imply that cash-based profitability is “pure cash flow” unless the paper defines it that way.

## 7.3 Results requiring verification

Working extraction notes include:

- Traditional B/M long–short return for the full sample around 0.79% per month, with instability across subperiods and substantial dependence on the short side.
- Traditional value after 2006 around 0.18% per month.
- CBOP/M high decile return around 0.584% per month.
- CBOP/M low decile return around −0.592% per month.
- CBOP/M high-minus-low spread around 1.175% per month, t-statistic around 3.615.
- Risk-adjusted spread around 0.532% to 0.917% depending on the benchmark model.
- Stronger and more stable subperiod performance than B/M, including a reported subperiod result around 1.88% per month for 2011–2015.
- New Value Factor, NVF, average return around 0.831% per month.
- NVF correlation with HML around 0.461.
- Spanning alpha around 0.358% per month with t-statistic around 2.34.
- Fama–MacBeth premium around 0.47%, while HML becomes insignificant in the reported specification.
- Results remain across size terciles.
- Replacing HML with NVF improves model performance in reported GRS tests; for one Fletcher-type five-factor comparison, a p-value reportedly rises from about 0.232 to about 0.620.

Do not place all these numbers on one slide. Use only the minimum numbers needed to establish each claim. Put detailed values in the appendix evidence table.

- Labels: one or two lines per node.

- Font: Source Sans 3 or Arial fallback.
- Flat fills, 1–1.5 pt strokes, no gradients, shadows, 3D shapes, or glossy effects.
- Use rounded corners sparingly; most research-process nodes should be simple rectangles.
- Use the deck palette and one accent per diagram.
- Export with transparent or warm-paper-compatible background.

Create these diagrams:

1. `DGM-01 — Research lineage and contribution map`
2. `DGM-02 — From operating profit to CBOP/M`

4. `DGM-04 — Evidence ladder and model-testing architecture`

Validate that the generated `.drawio` XML opens correctly and that all text remains editable.

# 14. Visual design system

## 14.1 Design synthesis

Use a disciplined hybrid:

- **FPT reference:** within-slide composition, title hierarchy, evidence density, and clear section rhythm.
- **SSI reference:** analyst-style sourcing, chart placement, navigation, and disciplined finance-deck structure.
- **Portfolio Management reference:** connection to the course’s existing visual language where appropriate.
- **Financial Times inspiration:** warm-paper palette, editorial restraint, chart grammar, typographic hierarchy, and semantic color.
- **Academic/LaTeX influence:** equations, footnotes, definitions, and scholarly credibility.
- **Consulting influence:** answer-first titles, one claim per slide, direct labels, and compact bottom-line statements.

The result must be **FT-inspired, not FT-branded**. Do not use the FT masthead, logo, protected brand claims, or imply affiliation.

## 14.2 Palette

Core colors:

- Warm paper background: `#FFF1E0` or a very close print-safe variant.
- Primary ink: `#262220`.
- Secondary ink: `#66605B`.
- Light rule/tint: `#E7DCCD`.
- Muted gold emphasis: approximately `#C5A253`.

Semantic colors, used only when meaning requires them:

- Benchmark/reference blue: `#2E6E9E`.
- Positive/constructive green-teal: approximately `#2F7D4F`.
- Negative/risk/low portfolio claret: `#9E2F50`.

Rules:

- Default to warm paper, ink, and one accent.
- Use claret only for negative/risk/low-leg meaning.
- Use green only for positive/high-leg or confirmed evidence.
- Never use multiple bright accents simply to distinguish categories.
- Maintain accessible contrast.

## 14.3 Typography

Use Canva-safe fonts:

- Preferred headline: `Newsreader`.
- Alternative headline: `Libre Baskerville` or `Georgia`.
- Body and labels: `Source Sans 3`.
- Fallback body: `Arial` or `Aptos`.
- Equations/numeric appendix: use the headline serif or a compatible math font; avoid a typewriter aesthetic.

Suggested size hierarchy for 16:9:

- Cover title: 34–44 pt depending on line count.
- Action title: 27–34 pt, maximum two lines.
- Lead/subtitle: 16–20 pt.
- Body: 15–19 pt.
- Chart labels: minimum 11–12 pt after import.
- Source/footer: minimum 8.5–10 pt.

Do not reduce text below readable sizes to save a crowded slide. Split or move detail to the appendix.

## 14.4 Grid and layout

- Use a consistent 12-column grid.
- Safe outer margins: approximately 0.45–0.60 inches.
- Reserve a stable top zone for the action title and optional lead-in.
- Reserve a stable bottom zone for source, paper title shorthand, and page number.
- Prefer editorial asymmetry over perfect centered symmetry.
- Most slides should have one dominant evidence region and one supporting interpretation region.
- Use generous whitespace, but not empty “luxury” whitespace that forces tiny content.
- Vary composition purposefully across slides while preserving the grid.

## 14.5 Sources and navigation

Every factual slide must include a small source line. Use formats such as:

`Source: Le Quy Duong, “A New Value Strategy in the UK Stock Market,” Table X, p. Y.`

For external research:

`Source: Author(s) (Year), Journal/Working Paper, DOI or stable URL.`

Use a discreet footer with slide number and abbreviated deck title. A subtle section marker may be used, but do not create a thick corporate navigation bar that competes with the academic content.

# 15. Anti-AI and anti-uncanny-valley rules

The deck must not look like a generic AI-generated presentation.

Strictly avoid:

- Dark blue/purple gradients.
- Glassmorphism, glow, neon, or “futuristic finance” decoration.
- Bento grids on every page.
- Repeated three-card layouts.
- Perfectly symmetrical blocks without an analytical reason.
- Rounded rectangles as the default container for every sentence.
- Icon soup, emojis, cartoon illustrations, and generic stock photos.
- Fake newspapers or FT branding.
- Decorative precision, such as unsupported exact numbers or arbitrary mini charts.
- Huge quotation marks, oversized icons, and generic “lightbulb” imagery.
- Topic-label titles such as “Methodology,” “Results,” or “Literature Review.”
- Dense bullet walls.
- Full paragraphs copied from the paper.
- More than one major claim per slide.
- Tables built from dozens of ungrouped shapes when a native editable table works.
- Full-slide screenshots or flattened slide images.
- Inconsistent margins, type scales, source formats, or chart colors.
- Decorative arrows that do not encode direction or causality.
- Legends when direct labels are possible.

Human-authored cues to preserve:

- Specific action titles.
- Unequal but balanced layouts.
- Deliberate editorial hierarchy.
- Small source notes.
- Real caveats.
- Selective emphasis rather than highlighting everything.
- Slide-to-slide rhythm: puzzle, mechanism, method, evidence, judgment.

# 16. Speaker allocation and delivery architecture

Do not divide the deck equally by slide count. Divide it according to energy, cognitive load, and presentation function.

## Speaker 1 — Hook and research puzzle

- Slides 1–4.
- Target time: approximately 3.5–4 minutes.
- Delivery: energetic, curious, confident.
- Job: make the audience care, establish the puzzle, and position the contribution.
- Do not spend time on equations.

## Speaker 2 — Concept and methodology translator

- Slides 5–8.
- Target time: approximately 4–5 minutes.
- Delivery: calm, precise, teacher-like.
- Job: explain CBOP intuition, data, portfolio construction, and why each test exists.
- This speaker should be the clearest technical explainer, not merely the person assigned the “dry” section.

## Speaker 3 — Evidence, critique, and closing

- Slides 9–14.
- Target time: approximately 5–6 minutes.
- Delivery: analytical at first, increasingly decisive, then measured in the critique.
- Job: explain the central results, evaluate robustness, state limitations, and close the argument.
- This speaker should lead general Q&A because their section integrates evidence and judgment.

## Q&A ownership

- Speaker 1 leads questions about motivation, contribution, and literature positioning.
- Speaker 2 leads questions about variables, portfolio formation, model mechanics, and data timing.
- Speaker 3 leads questions about numerical results, robustness, limitations, implementation, and interpretation.

## Script standards

- Aim for natural spoken English at approximately 115–135 words per minute.
- Total core script should fit 13–15 minutes, excluding Q&A.
- Do not simply read visible slide text.
- Explain intuition before technical labels.
- State only the few numbers that matter aloud.
- Round oral numbers sensibly while preserving exact numbers in slide labels/notes.
- Define unfamiliar terms the first time they appear.
- Use “the paper finds” rather than “we find” unless clearly describing the student team’s interpretation.
- Use “we interpret” for the team’s judgment.
- Avoid exaggerated phrases such as “revolutionary,” “groundbreaking,” or “guaranteed alpha.”

# 18. Reviewer mode

Run a constructive reviewer pass before finalizing the deck.

## 18.1 Contribution test

Ask:

- What is inherited from Wang 2024?
- What is specifically new in the UK application?
- Does the paper add new data, tests, factors, or interpretation?
- Is “new value strategy” a new economic mechanism or a new implementation of an existing measure?

Reflect the answer accurately on slide 4 and in Q&A.

## 18.2 Identification and design test

Check:

- Accounting-data publication lag and look-ahead protection.
- Delisting-return handling.
- Survivorship and sample filters.
- Choice of July formation date.
- Value weighting versus equal weighting.
- Microcap and liquidity exposure.
- Industry concentration.
- Whether the apparent premium comes mainly from the short side.
- Whether subperiods were pre-specified.
- Whether multiple model/measure searches could inflate significance.

## 18.3 Implementability test

Check whether the paper reports:

- Turnover.
- One-way and round-trip cost assumptions.
- Bid–ask spreads.
- Market impact.
- UK stamp duty or SDRT.
- Stock-borrow availability and shorting cost.
- Capacity.
- Long-only alternatives.
- Break-even transaction cost.

Do not accuse the paper of ignoring an item if it addresses it. If it does not, describe it as an open implementation question.

## 18.4 Alternative explanation test

Consider:

- Known profitability factors.
- Investment factors.
- Distress/default risk.
- Size and liquidity.
- Intangible capital.
- Sector structure.
- Accounting quality.
- Time-varying exposure or crisis regimes.

Recommend only tests that logically connect to the focal construct.

## 18.5 External-validity test

Consider:

- Genuine future holdout after publication.
- Replication in another developed market.
- International developed-market panel.
- Regime-specific evidence.
- Long-only implementation.
- Industry-neutral and liquidity-screened implementations.

# 19. Q&A defense bank

Prepare at least twelve likely lecturer questions and concise model answers. Include these topics unless the paper makes one irrelevant:

1. Why is CBOP/M considered a value measure rather than only a profitability measure?
2. What exactly does CBOP remove or adjust relative to operating profit?
3. Why scale by market equity rather than book assets or book equity?
4. What is genuinely new relative to Wang 2024?
5. Why is the UK an informative test market?
6. Is the premium driven by the long leg or the short leg?
7. Could small firms, liquidity, distress, or sectors explain the result?
8. How does NVF differ from HML and RMW?
9. What do spanning, Fama–MacBeth, and GRS tests each tell us?
10. Why does a higher GRS p-value not automatically prove the model is economically superior?
11. Could transaction costs, stamp duty, and short-sale costs eliminate the premium?
12. Is the paper truly out-of-sample, and what would a stronger holdout test look like?
13. How are reporting lags and look-ahead bias handled?
14. Why use value-weighted portfolios and annual rebalancing?
15. How would intangible-capital adjustments change the interpretation?
16. What single extension would most strengthen the paper?

For every answer:

- Give a 20–40 second oral response.
- Cite the exact paper or external source.
- State uncertainty honestly.
- Identify the best responding speaker.
- Identify an appendix slide if useful.
- Include a “do not say” warning where students may be tempted to overclaim.

# 21. Production workflow

Complete the following phases in order. Do not ask the user to approve intermediate phases.

## Phase 1 — Input audit

- Confirm which expected files are present and readable.
- Identify the focal paper.
- Identify design references versus research sources.
- Record optional missing files and the fallback used.

## Phase 2 — Focal-paper reconstruction

- Read the entire paper.
- Extract research question, hypotheses if stated, sample, variable definitions, portfolio construction, factor construction, benchmark models, tables, main findings, robustness, limitations, and implications.
- Build an internal evidence matrix with claim, number, test, source, and interpretation.

## Phase 3 — Author corpus analysis

- Read the supplied related papers selectively but sufficiently.
- Extract recurring research priorities and testing patterns.
- Do not expand into unrelated summaries.

## Phase 4 — Current literature update

- Search foundational, closest-prior, and recent extension literature.
- Distinguish published research from working papers.
- Select only literature relevant to contribution, limitations, or extensions.

## Phase 5 — Narrative architecture

- Confirm the central thesis.
- Write the 14 action titles.
- Ensure each slide has one principal claim.
- Check that slide order answers puzzle → mechanism → method → evidence → judgment.

## Phase 6 — Exhibit planning

- Assign placeholder IDs.
- Select chart types using analytical purpose.
- Extract exact numbers and sources.
- Draft draw.io diagrams in landscape form.

## Phase 7 — Deck creation

- Build the editable Canva deck if available.
- Otherwise build the editable, Canva-importable PPTX.
- Add source lines, page numbers, speaker notes, and placeholder briefs.

## Phase 8 — Script and Q&A creation

- Write the full three-speaker script.
- Check speaking time.
- Add handoffs, cues, short route, extended route, and anticipated questions.

## Phase 9 — Reviewer pass

- Challenge novelty, methodology, robustness, interpretation, and implementation.
- Revise the deck where the reviewer identifies an overclaim.
- Preserve a fair account of what the paper already addresses.

## Phase 10 — Visual and factual QA

- Render or preview every slide.
- Check cropping, overlap, alignment, font substitution, contrast, source readability, and placeholder size.
- Verify every number against the source ledger.
- Verify that draw.io files open.
- Verify that all output links and files are real.
- Verify that the main script fits the target time.

# 22. Acceptance rubric

Do not call the work complete until it passes this rubric.

## Research fidelity — 30 points

- Correct focal paper and construct.
- Correct sample and portfolio formation.
- Correct numbers and test interpretations.
- Clear separation of evidence, interpretation, and extension.
- No fabricated sources, data, page numbers, or replication claims.

## Narrative and pedagogy — 20 points

- Strong research puzzle.
- Clear CBOP intuition.
- Method understandable to Portfolio Management students.
- Evidence ordered logically.
- Fair, memorable conclusion.

## Visual system — 15 points

- FT-inspired but not branded.
- Academic and editorial, not generic AI.
- Consistent grid, hierarchy, typography, palette, and footers.
- Readable at presentation distance.
- Fully editable.

## Exhibit briefs and draw.io — 15 points

- Every placeholder is build-ready.
- Correct chart type and data specification.
- No fake quantitative visuals.
- Landscape draw.io workflows are valid and editable.

## Script and three-speaker delivery — 10 points

- Natural spoken English.
- Appropriate timing and energy arc.
- Clear handoffs.
- Does not read the slide verbatim.
- Contains 10-minute and 20-minute options.

## Reviewer and Q&A quality — 10 points

- Respectful but substantive critique.
- Recent, relevant literature.
- Implementability addressed.
- At least twelve defensible answers.

### Automatic failure conditions

Any of the following requires correction before delivery:

- Fabricated Canva link.
- Fabricated data or citation.
- Treating Wang 2024’s construct as entirely invented by the focal paper.
- Claiming net implementable alpha without costs and turnover.
- Calling a paper-derived reconstruction “EDA” or “replication.”
- Unreadable text or clipped content.
- Flattened slides that cannot be edited.
- Generic AI visual style.
- Missing script, missing placeholders, or missing source ledger.
