02 / Construct & Design · Accounting Mechanics

CBOP purges working-capital accruals from operating profit.

Step 1: OP = Revenue − COGS − SG&A   |   Step 2: CBOP = OP − ΔAR − ΔInventory − ΔPrepaid + ΔDeferred Rev + ΔAP + ΔAccrued Exp
Ball et al. (2016) Construct
WORKING-CAPITAL ACCRUAL ADJUSTMENTS (Δ = CHANGE OVER FY)
Accounting Line Item Sign Economic Rationale
Δ Accounts Receivable (ΔAR) − Deduct Booked credit sales not yet collected in cash.
Δ Inventory (ΔINV) − Deduct Cash tied up in unsold goods sitting in warehouses.
Δ Prepaid Expenses − Deduct Cash paid out in advance of expense recognition.
Δ Deferred Revenue + Add back Upfront cash received from customers before delivery.
Δ Accounts Payable (ΔAP) + Add back Operating inputs received without immediate cash outflow.
Δ Accrued Expenses + Add back Non-cash expense accruals not yet paid in cash.

Note: Following Ball et al. (2016), missing working-capital subcomponents in Datastream are set to zero (Paper p. 4).

Two distinct constructions: 10-decile trading spread vs. 2×3 asset-pricing factor.

RHigh−Low,t = RDecile 1,t − RDecile 10,t

CONSTRUCTION B · 2×3 INDEPENDENT FACTOR SORT (NVF)Tables 2, 9, 10, 12

Fama–French (1993, 2015) Size-Neutralized 2×3 Grid

Independent sort on Median Market Cap (50/50) and CBOP/M (30% High / 40% Neutral / 30% Low):

Size Partition High 30% (Value) Middle 40% (Neutral) Low 30% (Growth)
Small (Bottom 50% Cap) SV (Small High) SN (Small Neutral) SG (Small Low)
Big (Top 50% Cap) BV (Big High) BN (Big Neutral) BG (Big Low)
NVFt = ½ (BVt + SVt) − ½ (BGt + SGt) = +0.831%/mo
WHY DO THE NUMBERS DIFFER? (1.175% DECILE SPREAD VS. 0.831% NVF FACTOR MEAN)
1. Tail Cutoff Width: Decile spread takes the extreme 10% vs. 10% tails; NVF uses broader 30% vs. 30% breakpoints, diluting extreme tail returns.
2. Size Neutralization: Decile 1 and Decile 10 are single-sorted and value-weighted; NVF gives exact 50/50 equal weight to Small and Big value spreads.
3. Analytical Role: The 10-decile spread tests portfolio sorting power; NVF tests whether cash-based value acts as a systematic pricing factor.

PEDAGOGICAL DISTINCTION FOR NEW READERS

• Why is Portfolio Alpha (0.606% FF6) ≠ Spanning Alpha (0.358%)?
Portfolio Alpha regressions use the 10-decile H−L spread as LHS; Spanning regressions use the 2×3 NVF factor as LHS against 7 RHS factors.

• Why is Fama–MacBeth λ (0.466%) ≠ NVF Mean (0.831%)?
Fama–MacBeth estimates cross-sectional slope coefficients across individual stocks, controlling simultaneously for all other factor betas.

RIGOROUS ECONOMETRIC INTERPRETATION OF GRS

• What GRS p = 0.620 Means: Under the null hypothesis H0: α = 0, we fail to reject that the Fletcher + NVF factor tangency portfolio is mean-variance efficient for the 25 Size–B/M portfolios.

FOUR METHODOLOGICAL & IMPLEMENTATION BOUNDARIES IN DUONG (2025)
Critique Dimension Theoretical / Empirical Issue in Paper Direct Evidence in Paper Tables Consequence for Investors
1. Missing Signal Decomposition
OP/M vs. ΔWC/M		 Paper compares CBOP/M only against B/M. It never tests whether alpha comes from replacing Book Equity with flow operating earnings (OP/M) or from the working-capital accrual adjustment (ΔWC/M). Footnote 3 (p. 5) defines RMW via Gross Margin (GP/Rev) rather than OP/BE; RMW is weak in Table 10 (λ = 0.079%, t = 0.218). Cannot attribute how much of the +1.175% spread is pure earnings yield vs. cash accrual quality.
2. Intangible Capital SG&A Paradox
Peters & Taylor (2017)		 Pages 1–2 critique B/M for ignoring knowledge-economy intangibles. Yet OP = Rev − COGS − SG&A deducts 100% of SG&A, which includes R&D, software, and human-capital investment. Section 2 (p. 4) formula treats all SG&A as current period cost rather than capitalizing knowledge investments. Asset-light R&D innovators look artificially unprofitable and risk being pushed into short deciles.
3. Big-Cap Alpha Decay & Short-Leg Reliance
Table 6 & Table 11		 50.4% of the raw spread (−0.592% of 1.175%) comes from shorting Decile 10 cash-burners. In the liquid Big-Cap tercile, Long-Leg (High-1) alpha weakens substantially. Table 11 Panel B: Big-Cap High-1 FF6 α = +0.181%* (t = 1.909) (insig. at 5%). Decile 10 has Avg CBOP/M = −0.454. Institutional long-only or large-cap funds capture a much smaller gross alpha than 1.175%.
4. UK Market Frictions & Signal Lag

Q1: Why is NVF mean (+0.831%) smaller than the CBOP/M decile spread (+1.175%)? Do not say one is "wrong" or net of costs. They use different portfolio breakpoints and weighting: the decile spread takes extreme 10% vs. 10% single-sorted value-weighted tails (Table 6), whereas NVF is a 2×3 independent sort using 30% vs. 30% CBOP/M breakpoints averaged equally across Small and Big size groups (Table 2).
Q2: Why do you say Big-Cap performance weakens when Table 11 shows +0.952%*** raw spread? Acknowledge that the H−L spread is indeed significant! We fully agree that the Big-Cap H−L spread is statistically significant (+0.952%*** raw; +0.418%** FF6 α). Our nuance is specifically on the Long-Only leg (High-1) under FF6 (Table 11 Panel B), where α drops from +0.457%** in Small stocks to +0.181%* (t = 1.909) in Big stocks—making long-only large-cap implementation sensitive to trading costs.
Q3: Doesn't Ball et al. (2016) already show CBOP beats OP? Why test OP/M in the UK? Do not dismiss Ball et al. (2016) or Wang (2024). Ball et al. (2016) deflate CBOP by Total Assets (a profitability anomaly), whereas this paper scales CBOP by Market Cap (a valuation multiple). Because CBOP/M = OP/M − ΔWC/M changes both the numerator and denominator relative to B/M, testing OP/M on the same UK sample quantifies the exact marginal contribution of the UK accrual adjustment.
Q4: Does GRS p = 0.620 prove that Fletcher 5F + NVF is the true UK asset-pricing model? Never equate failing to reject H0 with proving H0. In classical hypothesis testing, p = 0.620 means we fail to reject joint zero alphas on the 25 Size–B/M test portfolios. It shows strong relative improvement over HML (GRS drops from 1.208 to 0.923), but per Lewellen, Nagel & Shanken (2010), a complete validation requires testing on industry and accrual-sorted portfolios as well.
