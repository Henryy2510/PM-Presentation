### SLIDE 03 — CBOP Purges Working-Capital Accruals from Operating Profit
- **Visual Elements:**
  - **Top Formula Bar:**
    $$\text{Step 1: }\text{OP} = \text{Revenue} - \text{COGS} - \text{SG\&A} \quad \big| \quad \text{Step 2: }\text{CBOP} = \text{OP} - \Delta\text{AR} - \Delta\text{INV} - \Delta\text{Prepaid} + \Delta\text{DefRev} + \Delta\text{AP} + \Delta\text{AccruedExp}$$
  - **Left Table:** Line-by-line working-capital accrual sign table ($-\Delta\text{AR}, -\Delta\text{INV}, -\Delta\text{Prepaid}, +\Delta\text{DefRev}, +\Delta\text{AP}, +\Delta\text{AccruedExp}$).
  - **Right SVG Horizontal Bar Diagram:** Pedagogical comparison of **Firm A (Credit Stuffed: $\text{OP}=£20\text{m}, \Delta\text{AR}=+£12\text{m} \Rightarrow \text{CBOP}=£8\text{m}, \text{CBOP}/M=4\%$)** vs. **Firm B (Cash Backed: $\text{OP}=£20\text{m}, \Delta\text{AR}=0 \Rightarrow \text{CBOP}=£20\text{m}, \text{CBOP}/M=10\%$)** on identical $M = £200\text{m}$.
- **Speaker Script (1:05):**
  > "Why does $\text{CBOP}$ work better than accounting earnings or book equity? Look at the two-step formula at the top.
  > Step 1 starts with Operating Profit: Revenue minus COGS minus SG&A.
  > Step 2 converts accounting operating profit into cash-based operating profit by deducting non-cash working-capital increases—specifically changes in Accounts Receivable ($\Delta\text{AR}$), Inventory ($\Delta\text{INV}$), and Prepaid Expenses—while adding back changes in Deferred Revenue, Accounts Payable, and Accrued Expenses.
  > For anyone reading this paper for the first time, look at the visual comparison of Firm A and Firm B on the right:
  > Both firms have a Market Cap of £200 million and report the exact same accounting Operating Profit of £20 million (an $\text{OP}/M$ yield of $10\%$).
  > However, Firm A booked £12 million of its sales on credit that customers haven't paid yet ($\Delta\text{Receivables} = +£12\text{m}$). Its true cash operating profit is only £8m, giving a $\text{CBOP}/M$ ratio of $4\%$.
  > Firm B collected $100\%$ of its sales in cash ($\Delta\text{Receivables} = 0$). Its $\text{CBOP}$ is £20m, giving a $\text{CBOP}/M$ ratio of $10\%$.
  > $\text{CBOP}/M$ immediately separates real cash generators from firms stuffing their income statements with uncollected accruals."

  > Look at the GRS formula at the top: GRS tests the joint null hypothesis $H_0$ that all 25 portfolio alphas equal zero simultaneously. A lower GRS statistic (and higher $p$-value) means the factor model’s tangency portfolio comes closer to spanning the efficient frontier.

### SLIDE 13 — Four Structural & Institutional Boundaries Qualify the Live Investment Claim
- **Visual Elements:**
  - **Top Decomposition Formula Bar:**
    $$\frac{\text{CBOP}_{i,t}}{M_{i,t-1}} \equiv \frac{\text{Rev}_{i,t} - \text{COGS}_{i,t} - \mathbf{100\%\cdot\text{SG\&A}_{i,t}}}{M_{i,t-1}} - \frac{\Delta\text{Working Capital Accruals}_{i,t}}{M_{i,t-1}} = \frac{\text{OP}_{i,t}}{M_{i,t-1}} - \frac{\Delta\text{WC}_{i,t}}{M_{i,t-1}}$$
  - **4-Row Diagnostic Critique Table:**
