# Reviewer Assessment and Lecturer Q&A

## Constructive reviewer assessment

**Contribution.** Duong (2025) provides UK evidence for a strategy inherited from Ball et al. (2016) and Wang (2024). The most defensible framing is a market-specific empirical extension: a new-country sample, return comparisons, factor spanning/pricing tests, size robustness and GRS comparisons. Do not call CBOP/M an entirely new construct.

**Design.** The paper uses LSPD returns and Datastream accounting data for approximately 1,700 non-financial UK LSE firms, June 1995–June 2024 for accounting coverage and July 2000–June 2024 for return tests. It includes delisted firms where available. The July portfolio sorts and prior fiscal-year-end signals are described, but an explicit point-in-time publication lag by accounting field should be clarified for replication.

**Evidence.** The full-period B/M high-minus-low return is 0.79% monthly, with most return from the low-B/M short leg; the 2006–24 B/M mean is 0.18%. CBOP/M has a reported 1.175% monthly high-minus-low return (t=3.615), and positive alphas under all displayed benchmarks. NVF has a spanning alpha and a cross-sectional premium; the GRS comparison improves when replacing HML with NVF in the tested set of 25 size-B/M portfolios.

**Main unresolved issues.** The paper tests size partitions but does not report realized turnover, execution costs, short borrow availability/costs, capacity or tax-adjusted net returns. The conclusion acknowledges transaction costs and income taxes are not addressed, while reasoning annual rebalancing should make them limited. Treat that as a claim to test, not as a measured conclusion. The paper also calls for work distinguishing risk from behavioral explanations. Further useful checks include industry-neutral and liquidity-screened sorts, point-in-time accounting release dates, alternate CBOP definitions, and a true post-publication holdout.

**Claims to avoid.** “Value is dead”; “CBOP is pure cash flow”; “the UK result is a clean independent replication”; “the strategy earns 1.175% net”; “NVF proves the asset-pricing model”; “a higher GRS p-value proves economic superiority”; or “annual rebalancing makes transaction costs minimal” as an established result.

## Twelve likely lecturer questions

### 1. Why is CBOP/M a value measure rather than only profitability?
**Answer (20–40 sec):** CBOP/M puts an operating-profitability measure over market capitalization. The numerator represents a cash-based, accrual-adjusted fundamental; the denominator represents the price investors pay. The paper therefore interprets it as a fundamental-to-price signal. It also contains profitability information, so the cleanest description is a profitability-to-market-value strategy used as a value signal. **Lead:** Speaker 2. **Open:** A1. **Source:** Duong Section 3.2, PDF p. 5; Wang (2024).

### 2. What does CBOP adjust relative to operating profit?
**Answer:** It adds changes in deferred revenue, accrued expenses and accounts payable, and subtracts changes in prepaid expense, inventory and accounts receivable. Those adjustments move operating profit toward a cash-based measure by adjusting working-capital accrual components. The paper follows Ball et al.; it does not define CBOP as literal cash receipts less all cash payments. **Lead:** Speaker 2. **Open:** A1. **Source:** Duong Section 3.2, PDF p. 5; Ball et al. (2016).

### 3. Why scale by market capitalization rather than assets or book equity?
**Answer:** The paper wants a fundamental-to-price comparison: CBOP captures an operating signal, while market capitalization is the equity market value investors pay. Other denominators would define different signals and are reasonable robustness checks, but they are not the measure tested in this paper. **Lead:** Speaker 2. **Open:** A1/A2. **Source:** Duong Section 3.2, PDF p. 5.

### 4. What is genuinely new relative to Wang (2024)?
**Answer:** The CBOP/price strategy is not newly invented in the UK paper. Wang studies US stocks; Duong tests the strategy in the UK and adds factor tests and size/model robustness for that market. We should call that a market-specific extension and factor evaluation, rather than claim theoretical novelty for the underlying ratio. **Lead:** Speaker 1. **Open:** A4. **Source:** Duong Section 2, PDF pp. 2–3; Wang, RAPS (2024), DOI 10.1093/rapstu/raad014.

### 5. Why is the UK an informative test market?
**Answer:** It is a major developed equity market with a different industry mix and its own prior value evidence; the paper identifies the UK as a gap in tests of Wang’s strategy. That makes it useful external-market evidence. It does not by itself establish that the result generalizes to all developed markets. **Lead:** Speaker 1. **Open:** A4. **Source:** Duong Abstract/Sections 1–2; Fletcher et al. (2024), DOI 10.1080/1351847X.2024.2312203.

### 6. Is the premium driven by the long leg or the short leg?
**Answer:** For B/M over the full period, most of the 0.79% spread comes from the low-B/M short leg: high B/M returns 0.15% monthly and low B/M −0.64%. For CBOP/M, the paper describes a balance between the +0.584% high decile and −0.592% low decile. That distinction matters for feasibility, because shorting costs and availability are not tested. **Lead:** Speaker 3. **Open:** A3. **Source:** Duong Tables 3 and 6, PDF pp. 8 and 12.

### 7. Could size, liquidity, distress or industries explain the result?
**Answer:** Size alone does not remove the pattern: the paper double-sorts on size and CBOP/M and reports positive adjusted spreads in small, neutral and big groups. That does not settle liquidity, distress, capacity or industry composition, which are separate exposures. Industry-neutral and liquidity-screened portfolios would be useful next tests. **Lead:** Speaker 3. **Open:** A5. **Source:** Duong Table 11, PDF p. 17.

### 8. How does NVF differ from HML and RMW?
**Answer:** HML is high-minus-low book-to-market; RMW is robust-minus-weak profitability; NVF is high-minus-low CBOP/market capitalization. NVF is positively correlated with HML at 0.461, so it overlaps, but the correlation is not one. It is a price-scaled cash-based profitability signal, distinct from the paper’s gross-profitability RMW construction. **Lead:** Speaker 2. **Open:** A2/A3. **Source:** Duong Sections 3.3 and Table 2, PDF pp. 5–7.

### 9. What do spanning, Fama–MacBeth and GRS each tell us?
**Answer:** Spanning asks whether existing factors explain the return of the candidate factor; NVF’s reported alpha is 0.358% monthly, t=2.344. Fama–MacBeth estimates whether factor exposures carry a cross-sectional premium; joint NVF premium is 0.466%, t=4.323. GRS tests whether a set of test-asset alphas is jointly zero under a model. These answer different questions and none alone establishes causal mechanism or implementable return. **Lead:** Speaker 2. **Open:** A3. **Source:** Duong Tables 9, 10, 12; Gibbons et al. (1989).

### 10. Why does a higher GRS p-value not prove the model is economically better?
**Answer:** A higher p-value means the test gives less evidence to reject the model’s joint zero-alpha restriction for the chosen test assets and sample. It is not a probability that the model is true, a direct measure of investor utility, or proof of superior returns. Here the Fletcher five-factor p-value rises from 0.232 to 0.620 after replacing HML with NVF; that supports improved fit in this test, subject to those limits. **Lead:** Speaker 3. **Open:** A3. **Source:** Duong Table 12, PDF p. 18.

### 11. Could transaction costs, stamp duty and short-sale costs eliminate the premium?
**Answer:** The paper does not measure net returns, turnover or borrow costs, and explicitly says transaction costs and income taxes are not addressed. It argues annual rebalancing should limit costs, but that is not a cost estimate. We need historical holdings, turnover, bid–ask and impact estimates, shorting availability/fees, applicable levies and capacity constraints to test whether the gross premium survives. **Lead:** Speaker 3. **Open:** A6. **Source:** Duong Conclusion, PDF p. 18; FCA (2021), Occasional Paper 60.

### 12. Is the paper truly out of sample, and what would be a stronger holdout?
**Answer:** The UK market is a different market from Wang’s US setting, so the evidence is external-market evidence. But the period is not a forward holdout after publication: the return sample ends June 2024 and the article was accepted May 2025. A stronger test would preregister the signal and portfolio rules, use point-in-time accounting releases, and evaluate a future period or another independent market after the design is fixed. **Lead:** Speaker 1. **Open:** A5. **Source:** Duong article metadata and Section 3.1, PDF pp. 1, 4.
