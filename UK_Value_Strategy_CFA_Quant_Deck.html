• Why Fletcher et al. (2024) for the UK? In UK equities, asset growth ($\text{CMA}$) is weak ($t=1.425$). Fletcher replaces $\text{RMW}$ and $\text{CMA}$ with financing-based investment factors: Composite Share Issuance ($\text{CSI}$) and Net Share Issuance ($\text{NSI}$).
• Decision Rule: If $\alpha_{\text{High}-\text{Low}} > 0$ and $t(\alpha) > 2.58$, abnormal return cannot be explained by existing risk premia.
PILLAR 2: FACTOR SPANNING REGRESSIONS (FAMA-FRENCH, 2018) TABLE 9
$$\text{NVF}_t = \alpha_{\text{span}} + \sum_{k=1}^{K} \gamma_k F_{k,t} + u_t \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \Delta\left(\text{Max Sharpe}^2\right) = \frac{\alpha_{\text{span}}^2}{\sigma_u^2}$$
• Tests whether $\text{NVF}$ lies inside the linear span of $\{\text{MKT}, \text{SMB}, \text{HML}, \text{RMW}, \text{CMA}, \text{WML}, \text{CSI}, \text{NSI}\}$. A statistically significant intercept $\alpha_{\text{span}} > 0$ proves $\text{NVF}$ strictly expands the tangency portfolio's Sharpe ratio.
PILLAR 3: FAMA-MACBETH (1973) CROSS-SECTIONAL REGRESSIONS TABLE 10
$$\text{Step 2 (Each month } t\text{): } R_{i,t} - R_{f,t} = \lambda_{0,t} + \lambda_{\text{NVF},t}\hat{\beta}_{i,\text{NVF}} + \lambda_{\text{HML},t}\hat{\beta}_{i,\text{HML}} + \sum_{k} \lambda_{k,t}\hat{\beta}_{i,k} + \eta_{i,t}$$ $$\hat{\lambda}_k = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T} \lambda_{k,t}, \qquad t(\hat{\lambda}_k) = \frac{\hat{\lambda}_k}{\text{SE}(\lambda_{k,t}) / \sqrt{T}}$$
• Runs across all individual stocks $i$ rather than aggregated portfolios, preventing loss of cross-sectional information and running a direct horse-race between $\hat{\beta}_{i,\text{NVF}}$ and $\hat{\beta}_{i,\text{HML}}$.
PILLAR 4: GIBBONS-ROSS-SHANKEN (GRS, 1989) EFFICIENCY TEST TABLE 12
$$\text{GRS} = \left(\frac{T}{N}\right)\left(\frac{T - N - L}{T - L - 1}\right) \left[\frac{\hat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}' \hat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}^{-1} \hat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}{1 + \bar{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_f' \hat{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}^{-1} \bar{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_f}\right] \sim F_{N,\, T-N-L}$$
• Evaluates $N=25$ Size-Value portfolios. Null Hypothesis $H_0: \alpha_1 = \dots = \alpha_{25} = 0$.
• Crucial Interpretation: A smaller $\text{GRS}$ statistic and larger $p\text{-value}$ ($p > 0.05$) mean joint pricing errors are closer to zero (superior factor model).

Slide 4 Speaker Script (~2.0 mins)

"On Slide 4, we formalize the four econometric tests used to validate CBOP/M. First, we run multi-factor time-series regressions against five asset pricing models, including Fletcher's (2021) UK-specific 5-factor models that incorporate Composite Share Issuance (CSI) and Net Share Issuance (NSI).

Second, we conduct Fama-French (2018) spanning regressions. Mathematically, the squared t-statistic of the spanning intercept alpha is proportional to the increase in the maximum squared Sharpe ratio of the factor tangency portfolio. Third, to ensure results are not an artifact of decile aggregation, we execute Fama-MacBeth (1973) cross-sectional regressions across all individual stocks. Finally, we run the Gibbons-Ross-Shanken (GRS) F-test on 25 Size-Value portfolios. Remember: in the GRS test, the null hypothesis is that all 25 pricing errors equal zero—so a LOWER GRS statistic and a HIGHER p-value signify a superior asset pricing model."

SECTION 4 • CONSTRUCTIVE CRITIQUE & METHODOLOGICAL BOUNDARIES

Five Structural Limitations: Accounting Paradoxes, Benchmark Design & Market Frictions

Critical Peer Review of Le Quy Duong (2025)
1. The Intangible Capital Paradox (Knowledge Economy Contradiction)
Pages 1–2 of the paper argue that $B/M$ failed because knowledge-economy firms invest heavily in intangibles (R&D, software, patents, human capital) omitted from balance-sheet book value. Yet the paper's own formula: $$\text{OP} = \text{Revenue} - \text{COGS} - \mathbf{\text{SG\&A}}$$ deducts 100% of $\text{SG\&A}$, which houses R&D, brand equity, and software development! By treating all intangible investments as immediate expenses rather than capital, $\text{CBOP}$ penalizes innovation-intensive tech/pharma firms, misclassifying them as "low-profitability" shorts (Peters & Taylor, 2017; Arnott et al., 2021).
2. Weak RMW Benchmark (Footnote 3) & Missing OP/M Horse-Race
• Footnote 3 (p. 5) Vulnerability: The paper constructs $\text{RMW}$ using Gross Profit Margin ($\text{GP}/\text{Revenue}$) instead of Fama-French (2015) Operating Profitability ($\text{OP}/\text{BE}$) or Novy-Marx (2013) $\text{GP}/\text{Assets}$. Margin captures sector pricing power rather than capital efficiency, weakening the RMW control ($t=0.218$ in Table 10).
• Missing Decomposition: Since $\frac{\text{CBOP}}{M} \equiv \frac{\text{OP}}{M} - \frac{\Delta\text{WC}}{M}$, omitting a direct horse-race against $\text{OP}/M$ leaves unproven how much alpha stems from cash accrual adjustment ($\Delta\text{WC}$) vs. simply replacing static $B$ with flow earnings ($\text{OP}$).
$$\text{Decomposition Identity: } \underbrace{\frac{\text{CBOP}_{i,t}}{M_{i,t}}}_{\text{Paper's Metric}} \equiv \underbrace{\frac{\text{OP}_{i,t} + \theta_{\text{int}}\text{SG\&A}_{i,t}}{M_{i,t}}}_{\text{Intangible-Adjusted Flow Yield}} - \underbrace{\frac{\Delta\text{WC}_{i,t}}{M_{i,t}}}_{\text{Cash Accrual Component}}$$
3. Unconditional Sorting & Cross-Sector Distortions
Sorting all non-financial firms unconditionally into deciles creates unintended sector bets. Structural working capital differs inherently across UK industries: • Retail & Supermarkets operate with negative working capital via large trade credit ($\Delta\text{AP} > 0$), mechanically boosting their $\text{CBOP}$.
• Manufacturing & Industrials require heavy inventories ($\Delta\text{INV} > 0$), mechanically depressing their $\text{CBOP}$. Without sector-neutralization, the portfolio makes unintended macroeconomic industry bets.
4. Econometric Inference in Fama-MacBeth (EIV & Noise)
• Errors-in-Variables (EIV): Table 10 runs 2nd-stage regressions on individual stocks using 1st-stage estimated betas ($\hat{\beta}_{i,k}$). This introduces EIV attenuation bias, requiring Shanken (1992) standard error corrections.
• Serial Correlation & Missing Data: Standard FM SEs ignore time-series persistence, requiring Newey-West HAC. Setting missing working capital components to zero (p. 4) injects measurement noise in early sample years.
5. Real-World Execution Frictions & Short-Leg Fragility
• UK Stamp Duty Reserve Tax (SDRT): Every UK equity purchase incurs a mandatory 50 bps ($0.50\%$) tax.
• Short-Leg Borrowing & Squeeze Risk: 50.4% of raw spread comes from shorting Decile 10 cash-burners ($\text{CBOP}/M = -0.454$), facing high borrow fees ($150–400\text{ bps/yr}$) and short squeezes.
• 11-Month Signal Staleness: Annual July rebalancing leaves financial statements stale by up to 11–15 months.
