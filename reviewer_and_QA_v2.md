# Reviewer and Q&A preparation (V2)

Use Appendix A8 to navigate. Answers are designed for about 20–40 seconds; keep the distinction between reported results and our interpretation clear.

1. **Is the 1.175% figure annual?** No. It is a monthly high-minus-low average return in Table 6. It is a gross portfolio result; the paper does not provide a cost-adjusted annual return. **Route:** slide 9, A3. **Boundary:** do not annualize arithmetically as realized investor performance.
2. **Is CBOP simply cash flow?** No. It adjusts operating profit for specified working-capital accrual changes. It is an accrual-adjusted operating-profit measure, not a statement-of-cash-flows total. **Route:** slide 5, A1. **Boundary:** retain the paper's exact variable names and signs.
3. **How exactly is CBOP/M formed?** Operating profit is revenue less COGS and SG&A. Add changes in deferred revenue, accrued expenses, and accounts payable; subtract changes in prepaid expense, inventory, and accounts receivable; then scale by market capitalization. **Route:** slide 5, A1–A2. **Boundary:** the detailed timing/definitions should follow the paper's appendix.
4. **Could the annual July sort use information not yet public?** The paper describes prior fiscal-year-end information and July formation. Its sample description does not spell out a security-level reporting lag. We therefore flag point-in-time availability as a replication detail to document, not as a proven bias. **Route:** slide 7, A2/A5. **Boundary:** avoid asserting either perfect lag control or look-ahead bias.
5. **Does including delisted firms solve survivorship bias?** It helps: the authors state delisted firms are included where available. It does not establish full coverage or eliminate all selection from missing data. **Route:** slide 6, A2. **Boundary:** phrase as mitigation, not resolution.
6. **Why call a profitability-to-price ratio a value strategy?** It is a fundamental operating measure scaled by market price. The paper tests whether this alternative fundamental-to-price signal captures returns that B/M misses. Whether it is best described as value or profitability is a construct question. **Route:** slides 4–5, A4. **Boundary:** distinguish the inherited signal from the UK contribution.
7. **Does alpha mean arbitrage?** No. Alpha is the intercept relative to a specified factor model. Omitted risks, data mining, borrow costs, liquidity, and trading costs can remain. **Route:** slide 10, A3/A5. **Boundary:** say “not absorbed by the tested model,” not “risk-free.”
8. **Does the factor add information beyond HML?** Within the reported tests, correlation is 0.461, the spanning intercept is 0.358% monthly (t=2.344), and the joint Fama–MacBeth NVF premium is 0.466% (t=4.323), while HML is 0.113% (t=1.576). This supports incremental sample evidence, not universal superiority. **Route:** slide 11, A3/A6. **Boundary:** do not infer causal mechanism.
9. **Does the higher GRS p-value prove NVF is better?** No. Replacing HML with NVF raises the reported GRS p-value in the stated model comparisons. That means less rejection evidence for those model/test-asset combinations; non-rejection is not proof. **Route:** A6. **Boundary:** keep model and test portfolios attached to the claim.
10. **What is the strongest next test?** A point-in-time, out-of-sample replication with explicit reporting lags, turnover, spread/impact estimates, short availability/fees, capacity and liquidity screens. Report gross and net results side by side. **Route:** slide 12, A5/A7. **Boundary:** this is our proposed research design, not a test already completed by the paper.
11. **Could intangibles explain the B/M weakness?** It is a plausible extension because conventional balance sheets may omit internally generated intangible investment. The focal study does not establish that mechanism. **Route:** A4–A5. **Boundary:** present as hypothesis and cite the external accounting/value literature in the ledger.
12. **Is the result monotonic?** The paper describes a monotone pattern, but adjacent reported averages are not strictly descending at every step: D2 and D3, for example, are close and not strictly ordered. The slide uses “strongly ordered” and shows every portfolio value. **Route:** slide 9, A3. **Boundary:** never claim perfect monotonicity.
